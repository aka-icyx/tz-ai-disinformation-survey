Community leaders in several regions are encouraging residents to make better use of newly improved road networks by establishing small businesses along key trade routes. They argue that better connectivity should translate into tangible economic benefits for local families rather than simply easing travel. "Good roads should open doors for farmers, traders and artisans to reach new markets," one local leader remarked. Social workers add that such initiatives, when paired with access to basic training and microfinance, could significantly boost household incomes and reduce dependency on subsistence farming in rural communities.

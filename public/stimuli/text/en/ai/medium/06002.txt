Cooperative savings groups are increasingly stepping in to fill gaps left by conventional lenders, particularly for small traders struggling to access affordable credit for stocking goods ahead of peak seasons. Members typically pool modest monthly contributions, then borrow against the collective fund at lower interest rates than commercial banks offer.

Economists note that while such groups cannot replace formal financial systems entirely, they provide a practical cushion for households facing rising costs of basic goods, helping many avoid predatory lenders who often charge exploitative rates during periods of financial strain.

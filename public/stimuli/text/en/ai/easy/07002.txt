Some private employers in urban areas are beginning to adjust workplace practices to better accommodate staff with visual and hearing impairments, offering flexible arrangements such as modified workstations and assistive communication tools. Disability advocates say these changes, though still limited in scope, represent a meaningful shift in attitudes toward inclusive employment.

Workers with disabilities note that beyond physical adjustments, colleagues' willingness to learn basic sign language or adapt communication styles has made a noticeable difference in daily workplace interactions, fostering a sense of belonging that many previously felt was missing from formal job environments.

Several villages in the Lake Zone are witnessing improved access to clean water following the installation of solar-powered boreholes, easing a burden that has long affected women and children who previously walked long distances daily. Local residents say the new systems have reduced water collection time significantly, allowing more hours for farming and small trade activities.

Unlike diesel-powered pumps common in remote areas, the solar systems require minimal maintenance and lower operating costs, making them more sustainable for community management committees tasked with overseeing daily upkeep and fair water distribution.
